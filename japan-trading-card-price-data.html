Japan Trading Card Price Data

The Japanese card market moves differently from the English-language one. Sealed boxes, single cards and promo distributions are often priced first in Japan, and an English-market repricing tends to follow rather than lead it.

Sampling Japanese listings in bulk gives you the leading edge of that series. The scraper returns one record per listing with the price, condition and image URL, so you can chart a card's Japanese ask against its overseas price.

Mandarake Auction Scraper → All Japanese market scrapers →

What you get

item_nameCard, box or product name as listed.
current_price_jpyAsking or cleared price in yen.
conditionGrade for singles where published.
image_urlListing image, useful for spotting reprints and counterfeit tells.
item_urlSource URL.

Field names follow the actor's dataset schema and can differ slightly between actors — check the actor page for the exact input and output schema before wiring it into a pipeline.

Use cases

  • Arbitrage research: compare the Japanese ask against the English-market ask for the same card.
  • Sealed product tracking: follow how box prices move as supply tightens.
  • Inventory review: batch-price a collection from a single search sweep.

FAQ

Can I get sold prices for cards?
Auction-sourced records keep an end timestamp, so closed lots can be separated from live asks.
Is grading data included?
Where the source publishes a grade it is captured in the condition field; the scrapers do not perform their own grading.
Does this cover English-language cards?
Coverage follows the Japanese marketplaces, which list both Japanese and imported product.
